Beowulf's Final Battle
(Sections XXXIV–XLIII)

The Dragon, Loyalty, and the End of a King

Group 5 — Tselmeg, Bilguudei, Bilguun, Purevbat (Class 10B)

Summary Part 1 — Facing the Dragon
Sections XXXIV–XXXVI

  • 50 Years Later: Elderly King Beowulf marches to the dragon's cave with 11 warriors.
  • Fighting Alone: Beowulf orders his men to stay back so none of them get killed.
  • The First Clash: The dragon blasts fire; Beowulf's sword (Naegling) fails against its hard scales.
  • Betrayal: Terrified by the flames, 10 warriors abandon their king and run into the woods.

Summary Part 2 — Loyalty & Victory
Sections XXXVII–XXXIX

  • Wiglaf Steps In: Young warrior Wiglaf refuses to run and charges into the fire to help.
  • The Mortal Wound: Beowulf's sword breaks; the dragon bites his neck with deadly poison.
  • Defeating the Beast: While the dragon bites Beowulf, Wiglaf stabs its soft belly, and Beowulf cuts it down.
  • Dying Wishes: Beowulf passes his armor and leadership to Wiglaf and asks for a coastal burial mound.

Summary Part 3 — The Aftermath & Prophecy
Sections XL–XLIII

  • Wiglaf's Rebuke: Wiglaf angrily scolds the 10 fleeing cowards, telling them dishonor is worse than death.
  • The Messenger's Warning: A messenger announces Beowulf's death and warns that enemies like the Franks and Swedes may now attack Geatland.
  • The Dragon's Hoard: The cursed gold treasure is buried alongside Beowulf so no one profits from it.
  • The Pyre: The Geats burn Beowulf's body on a massive pyre and build a high memorial mound by the sea.

Character & Figure Spotlight
Key Figures in the Final Battle

Beowulf

Aged King of Geatland; courageous and selfless, meeting his heroic fate.

Wiglaf

Loyal kinsman and young warrior who steps up when others flee.

The Dragon

Ancient guardian of cursed treasure; symbolizes destruction and fate.

The 10 Warriors

Fleeing retainers whose cowardice brings shame upon the Geat nation.

Literary Analysis & Themes
Deeper Meanings

  • Wyrd (Fate): Beowulf accepts his doom with dignity, knowing his final hour has come.
  • Comitatus Broken: The sacred bond between king and warrior fails when 10 men run.
  • Good King vs. Greedy Dragon: Beowulf gives his life to protect his people, while the dragon hoards useless gold.
  • Elegiac Tone: The text shifts from triumph to deep sorrow, foreshadowing the downfall of the Geats.

Conclusion & Q&A
The Legacy of Beowulf

  • The Final Legacy: Beowulf dies as a true protector, remembered forever as the greatest hero of the Geats.
