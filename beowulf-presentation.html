Beowulf’s Final Battle
(Sections XXXIV–XLIII)

The Dragon, Loyalty, and the End of a King

Group 5 — Tselmeg, Bilguudei, Bilguun, Purevbat (Class 10B)

An old king holding a shield and sword facing a fire-breathing dragon outside a stone cave

Summary Part 1 — Facing the Dragon
Sections XXXIV–XXXVI

  • 50 Years Later: Elderly King Beowulf marches to the dragon’s cave with 11 warriors.
  • Fighting Alone: Beowulf orders his men to stay back so none of them get killed.
  • The First Clash: The dragon blasts fire; Beowulf’s sword (Naegling) fails against its hard scales.
  • Betrayal: Terrified by the flames, 10 warriors abandon their king and run into the woods.
Diagram: Beowulf facing the dragon alone, 10 soldiers running toward the trees, 1 staying behind

Summary Part 2 — Loyalty & Victory
Sections XXXVII–XXXIX

  • Wiglaf Steps In: Young warrior Wiglaf refuses to run and charges into the fire to help.
  • The Mortal Wound: Beowulf’s sword breaks; the dragon bites his neck with deadly poison.
  • Defeating the Beast: While the dragon bites Beowulf, Wiglaf stabs its soft belly, and Beowulf cuts it in half.
  • Dying Wishes: Beowulf passes his armor and leadership to Wiglaf and asks for a coastal burial mound.
Two warriors working together to defeat a giant dragon, with a broken sword on the ground

Summary Part 3 — Aftermath & Funeral
Sections XL–XLIII

  • Shaming the Cowards: Wiglaf furiously condemns the 10 deserters for disgracing their king.
  • Warning of War: A messenger warns that enemy tribes will soon attack now that Beowulf is gone.
  • The Funeral Pyre: The Geats burn Beowulf’s body and push the dragon into the ocean.
  • Cursed Treasure: The people bury all the dragon's gold inside Beowulf’s tomb—useless in the ground.
A coastal burial mound overlooking the ocean, smoke rising from a funeral fire

Key Quotes from the Poem

Quote 1 — Beowulf on Fighting Alone (Section XXXV) The fight is not yours, nor is it up to any man except me to measure his strength against the monster.

Meaning: Shows Beowulf’s courage and desire to protect his soldiers from dying.

Quote 2 — Wiglaf on Loyalty vs. Cowardice (Section XXXIX) Death is better for any warrior than a life of disgrace!

Meaning: Wiglaf shames the 10 men who ran away, proving honor matters more than survival.

The Big Question & Conclusion

Is Beowulf’s battle with the dragon his greatest success or his greatest mistake?

  • Our Answer — Greatest Success
  • 50 Years of Rule: Beowulf already dominated as a peaceful, undefeated king for half a century.
  • Honorable Match: Sensing his death was near, he faced the deadliest enemy he ever knew and killed it—an honorable tie rather than dying weak in bed.
  • Saved His People: He stopped the dragon from destroying his kingdom and passed leadership to a worthy successor (Wiglaf).
Beowulf’s burial mound at sunset, or Wiglaf holding the king’s armor

Why This Section Matters: Completes Beowulf’s heroic journey and shows that true leadership means loyalty and sacrifice, not gold.
